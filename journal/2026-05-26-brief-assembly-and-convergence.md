# Session — Brief Assembly, Mom's Validation & Three-Voice Convergence (2026-05-26)

> Morning blend test validated. Partner package assembled (brief, brewing letter, back-of-bag, cover email). Three independent voices converged on brand positioning. Ask-first brief structure learned from Tatiana's correction. Panela vocabulary corrected. Daily consumption calculated at 86 bags/year.

## Observations

- **Daily consumption:** 80 g/day total (Chris + Tatiana) → 29.2 kg/year ≈ 86 bags of 12 oz. Prior subscriptions covered only ~42% of need.
- **The story (documentary, not marketing):** While dating, Chris generated ChatGPT cartoons of bringing her coffee with caption "algún día." After marriage, he makes her coffee every morning — chocolate-note base + ground cinnamon bark + panela/sugar. The 70/30 blend recreates that exact balance. Grown near Tatiana's hometown in Colombia.
- **Three-voice convergence:** Chris (strategic articulation), Tatiana mid-tasting ("we are sending romance"), Chris's mother by phone ("a special cup of coffee for everyday people"). Three vantages, three days, no coordination.
- **Mom validated $25 price point unprompted** and volunteered a customer-behavior analog (a specific tea at Yellow Jelly she returns to because she can't get it elsewhere).

## Decisions

- Brief and full Elkin package stay unchanged — mom's validation is strategic confidence to SEND, not new input to add
- "A special cup of coffee for everyday people" replaces "approachable luxury" as outward-facing positioning line

## Learnings

- **Ask-first brief structure:** Tatiana caught a critical framing error — the brief presumed custom blending rather than asking for it. Reframe: celebrate all four samples → name blend as discovery → ask explicitly if blending is available → name fallback. Section order: "What we want to launch" leads before "What we found."
- **Panela vocabulary correction:** "Piniella" was a phonetic invention from voice transcript; "piloncillo" is Mexican Spanish. Correct Colombian word is "panela." Error survived three encoding passes before being caught. Colombian Spanish vocabulary takes precedence in all communications.
- **Independent unprompted convergence from household is the strongest signal** — stronger than focus-group response from strangers
- **Errors compound across encoding passes when not caught early** — vocabulary verification should be a separate audit pass

## Constraints

- Colombian Spanish vocabulary takes precedence over generic or Mexican Spanish (partnership-respect constraint)

## Handoffs

- Partner package sent to Elkin: cover email, brief, brewing letter, back-of-bag (all EN+ES)
- Awaiting Elkin's pricing/blending reply
