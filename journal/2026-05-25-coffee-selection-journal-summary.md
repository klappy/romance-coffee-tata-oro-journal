## Executive Summary

Four candidate coffees (Castillo Chocolate, Bourbon Rosado Canela, Bourbon Ají Panela, Papayo Vanilla) narrowed to one launch product: a blend of Castillo Chocolate and Bourbon Rosado at 70/30 ratio, brewed pre-ground at finer-than-standard-drip grind, positioned at ~$25 retail. Papayo eliminated early (too intense for daily drinking). Bourbon Ají held in reserve. The blend is not a compromise — it is the only product satisfying three simultaneous constraints.

## The Three-Constraint Architecture

1. **Tatiana's preference** — she prefers Castillo solo. It's bolder, more daily-drinkable, more her.
2. **Audience price ceiling** — Rosado solo retails ~$40, unattainable for Tatiana's audience. Castillo solo at ~$20 is accessible.
3. **The brand's literal ritual** — for 18+ months, Chris grinds fresh cinnamon bark into Tatiana's coffee every morning. The Rosado's natural cinnamon character is exactly what that ritual tastes like. Castillo solo lacks the cinnamon. The story requires it.

The blend is the only product that gives Tatiana's preferred daily cup carrying the cinnamon that is the heart of the story, at a price the audience meets.

## Key Decisions (selected from 52+ decisions encoded)

- **D1:** Papayo Vanilla OUT — too intense, rejected by Tatiana as "too much"
- **D3:** Launch product is a BLEND, not single varietal — each coffee solo has a fatal weakness
- **D5:** Castillo roast level LOCKED at current spec — adjusting darker risks losing the smooth character Tatiana loved
- **D6:** Pre-ground bags ground finer than standard drip — calibrated for pour-over/Chemex, labeled as feature
- **D20:** All vocabulary corrected from "Piniella/piloncillo" to "Panela" (Colombian Spanish)

## Key Observations

- **Castillo Chocolate:** Smooth, approachable, Tatiana's preference for daily drinking. But alone, "nice but forgettable" — chocolate is a description, not a story.
- **Bourbon Rosado Canela:** Transcendent cinnamon character. Chris's first pour-over produced audible delight. But solo at ~$40 retail, it becomes "a coffee people only drink on Christmas" — breaks the daily ritual positioning.
- **Blend 70/30 (Castillo:Rosado):** Tested May 26 morning in Tatiana's drip pot. Tatiana asked for less sweetener than usual. Three of four success criteria met (positive reaction without prompting, canela identifiable, would choose over solo Castillo). The fourth (remains good as it cools) was not tested.
- **Creamy mouthfeel:** Tatiana discovered an unexpected body/texture in the blend that neither component has solo — described as "creamy." This became one of the three defensible product properties.

## Three Defensible Product Properties

1. **Mouthfeel** — the creamy body Tatiana discovered, unique to the blend
2. **Flavor** — chocolate base with cinnamon finish, the literal taste of the daily ritual
3. **Story** — documentary testimony, not marketing. The "algún día" cartoons → real daily coffee with ground cinnamon → the blend that IS that ritual
