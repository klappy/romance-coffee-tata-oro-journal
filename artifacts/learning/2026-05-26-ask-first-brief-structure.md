# Learning — Ask-First Brief Structure (2026-05-26)

> Tatiana caught a critical framing error in the Elkin brief: it presumed custom blending was available rather than asking whether it was. This correction became a structural principle for all partner communications. The brief was reframed: celebrate all four samples → name the blend as a discovery → ask explicitly if blending is available → name the fallback.

**Type:** Learning
**Quality:** Strong
**Source:** Tatiana's direct correction during brief review

## The Error

The original brief draft presented the 70/30 blend as a settled decision and asked Elkin for pricing on it — implying custom blending was already confirmed as a service. It was not. The brief was presuming a capability it had not verified.

## The Correction

Tatiana flagged it. The brief was restructured:

1. **"What we want to launch"** leads (ask-first executive shape)
2. **"What we found"** follows (discovery narrative)
3. **Celebrate all four samples** — honor the supplier's work before naming a preference
4. **Name the blend as a discovery** from those samples, not a demand
5. **Ask explicitly** if custom blending is available at 100-bag scale
6. **Name the fallback** — Castillo Chocolate solo if blending isn't feasible, Bourbon Rosado Canela held for a future seasonal SKU

## Structural Principle

Never presume a partner's capabilities. Frame asks as questions, not instructions. The section order of partner briefs matters: what you want comes before what you found, because the recipient needs to know why they're reading before they care about the details.
